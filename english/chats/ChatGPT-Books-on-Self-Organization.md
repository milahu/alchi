Yes. And the example you give points to a **more specific topic than ordinary “productivity.”** You seem interested in **self-organization as a system of recurring practices**: planning, coordination, reflection, feedback, adaptation, and gradually improving the way a person or team operates.

I’d divide the books into a few groups:

### 1. For personal self-organization

**David Allen - *Getting Things Done***  
Probably the first book I'd recommend. GTD is essentially a complete system for organizing commitments: capture → clarify → organize → reflect → act. The particularly relevant part for your example is the **regular review/reflection cycle**. ([Penguin Random House](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/the-read-down/books-to-read-after-atomic-habits/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

**James Clear - *Atomic Habits***  
Less about planning and more about **building the routines that make self-organization sustainable**. Your morning/evening meetings are themselves habits, and Clear's ideas are useful for designing them so they actually stick. ([Penguin Random House](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/the-read-down/books-to-read-after-atomic-habits/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

**David Allen's *Getting Things Done* + *Atomic Habits*** make a particularly good combination: the former gives you the **organizational system**, the latter helps you make the system **behaviorally sustainable**.

### 2. For exactly the kind of team practice you describe

This is where I think your question gets especially interesting.

**Tom Seeger - *Das agile Team steuert sich selbst***  
This is very close to what you're describing. It deals explicitly with **self-steering/self-organizing teams**, including retrospectives, team reflection, intervision, communication, and systemic thinking. ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-658-31171-1?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

**Roman Lobus - *The Art of Creating Self-Organizing Teams***  
A very recent (2026) practitioner-oriented book specifically about creating self-organizing teams. It covers team coaching, autonomy, ownership, team dynamics, and practical interventions. ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/979-8-8688-2151-6?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

**Siegfried Kaltenecker - *Selbstorganisierte Teams führen***  
Another German-language option, with quite a practical orientation. The material includes things such as **personal retrospectives, energy checks, colleague feedback, feed-forward, and dealing with difficult team situations**. ([Schweitzer Online](https://www.schweitzer-online.de/ebook/Kaltenecker/Selbstorganisierte-Teams-fuehren/9783969105337/A61259498/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

And there's an important connection to your example: Agile/Scrum deliberately creates recurring feedback loops-**Daily**, **Review**, and **Retrospective**-so that the team continually coordinates itself and adapts its way of working. ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-662-62231-5_2?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

### 3. For organizing work visually and collectively

**Jim Benson & Tonianne DeMaria Barry - *Personal Kanban: Mapping Work \| Navigating Life***

This one is interesting if you don't just want *meetings*, but want a **visible system around the meetings**.

Its two central ideas are:

- **Visualize your work**
- **Limit work in progress**

The result is a system where the team can actually see what it has committed to, what's happening now, and what's getting stuck. ([Personal Kanban](https://personalkanban.com/resources/books/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

I think this pairs extremely well with your morning/evening ritual.

For example:

**Morning**

> What are we going to accomplish today?

→ Look at the board → choose priorities → assign/coordinate.

**Evening**

> What happened? What did we learn? What should we change tomorrow?

→ Look at the board → review → adapt.

That's essentially a **feedback-control loop**.

### 4. If you're interested in the management/organizational side

**Boris Gloger & Dieter Rösner - *Selbstorganisation braucht Führung***

This is particularly good if your question is:

> “How can a leader create an environment in which people actually organize themselves, rather than the manager constantly organizing them?”

The book focuses on the conditions and tools needed for self-organization, rather than simply telling people to “take more responsibility.” ([Hanser Fachbuch](https://www.hanser-fachbuch.de/Selbstorganisation-braucht-Fuehrung/978-3-446-47269-3?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

That's an important distinction. **Self-organization isn't the absence of organization.** It usually means creating a system in which organization happens continuously *from within the group*.

## The concept I think you're looking for

Your morning/evening meeting can actually be understood as a very general pattern:

**1. Observe → 2. Decide → 3. Act → 4. Reflect → 5. Adjust**

Or:

``` text
          ┌───────────────┐
          │   PLAN        │
          │ What will     │
          │ we do today?  │
          └───────┬───────┘
                  ↓
             ┌─────────┐
             │   ACT   │
             └────┬────┘
                  ↓
          ┌───────────────┐
          │   REFLECT     │
          │ What happened?│
          │ What was good?│
          │ What was bad? │
          └───────┬───────┘
                  ↓
          ┌───────────────┐
          │   ADJUST      │
          │ What should   │
          │ we change?    │
          └───────┬───────┘
                  │
                  └──────→ PLAN
```

That idea appears under several different names:

- **self-organization**
- **self-management**
- **feedback loops**
- **continuous improvement**
- **reflection**
- **retrospectives**
- **cybernetic control**
- **agile ways of working**
- **personal organization**
- **organizational learning**

And I suspect **“feedback loops + self-organization”** is actually closer to what you're looking for than “productivity.”
