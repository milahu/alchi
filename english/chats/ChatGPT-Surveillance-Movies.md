Absolutely. If what you liked about **Enemy of the State** is specifically the *“the state has enormous surveillance capabilities, and the people operating them are willing to abuse them”* angle-not merely generic spy action-there's a very good rabbit hole to go down.

I'd divide the recommendations into a few flavors:

### 🛰️ Closest to *Enemy of the State*

1.  **The Conversation (1974)** - ★★★★★  
    The Conversation  
    **Probably the \#1 recommendation.** Gene Hackman plays a professional wiretapper who records a seemingly innocuous conversation and gradually realizes that his surveillance work may be facilitating a murder. It's much quieter and more psychologically disturbing than *Enemy of the State*, but the connection is extremely direct-Tony Scott essentially turned Hackman's surveillance expert into the much more action-oriented "Brill." ([Den of Geek](https://www.denofgeek.com/movies/best-90s-paranoid-political-thrillers/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

2.  **Three Days of the Condor (1975)** - ★★★★★  
    Three Days of the Condor  
    Robert Redford is a CIA analyst whose colleagues are mysteriously murdered, leaving him trying to figure out **which part of his own organization has gone rogue**. Excellent on the idea that intelligence agencies can become self-perpetuating machines whose employees don't even know what operation they're participating in. ([selections.rockefeller.edu](https://selections.rockefeller.edu/3-days-of-the-condor-the-parallax-view-and-the-conversation-1970s-cinema-mirrors-the-ascendancy-of-americas-conspiracy-obsession/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

3.  **The Parallax View (1974)** - ★★★★★  
    The Parallax View  
    This is considerably darker. A journalist investigates a political assassination and uncovers a shadowy organization involved in **recruitment, psychological conditioning and political assassination**. Very much the *“what if the conspiracy is institutional?”* movie.

4.  **The Manchurian Candidate (1962)** - ★★★★★  
    The Manchurian Candidate  
    Cold War psychological warfare, brainwashing, political manipulation and assassination. It's older and less technologically oriented, but its conception of **state actors using people as disposable instruments** is exactly in the territory you're describing.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 🔥 If you specifically want "active measures", assassinations and black operations

5.  **Syriana (2005)** - ★★★★★  
    Syriana  
    **Very strong recommendation for you.** Multiple intelligence, political and corporate operations intersect, including covert action, assassinations, informants, manipulation and plausible deniability. It's less "one guy gets chased by the NSA" and more *“this is what the machinery looks like from inside.”*

6.  **Munich (2005)** - ★★★★★  
    Munich  
    Probably the best movie on your list if **“silent kills / state-sponsored assassination”** is the particular attraction. It follows an Israeli covert assassination campaign and, importantly, doesn't portray the operation as clean or heroic. It becomes a meditation on what happens psychologically and politically when governments decide assassination is an instrument of policy.

7.  **The Good Shepherd (2006)** - ★★★★½  
    The Good Shepherd  
    CIA history from the inside, covering the OSS/CIA world, recruitment, counterintelligence, surveillance, coups and clandestine operations. **Slow, cold and cerebral**, rather than an action thriller.

8.  **Spy Game (2001)** - ★★★★½  
    Spy Game  
    This one is especially worth watching because it's **Tony Scott again**. Robert Redford and Brad Pitt, CIA operations, covert missions, political manipulation and the agency's willingness to sacrifice its own people. Less paranoid than *Enemy of the State*, but very much concerned with the morality of clandestine operations.

9.  **The Fourth Protocol (1987)** - ★★★★½  
    The Fourth Protocol  
    A more traditional Cold War espionage thriller, but excellent if you like the idea of intelligence services conducting **elaborate clandestine operations while political leaders remain several layers removed from what is actually happening**.

### 👁️ Surveillance / "you are being watched"

10. **The Lives of Others (2006)** - ★★★★★  
    The Lives of Others  
    Set in East Germany, where a Stasi officer monitors a playwright and his circle. No Hollywood action, but **one of the most powerful films ever made about what surveillance actually does to people**. The mechanics of bugging, informants, files and psychological pressure are fascinating.

11. **Snowden (2016)** - ★★★★  
    Snowden  
    Much more explicitly about modern NSA surveillance. It's essentially the real-world counterpart to *Enemy of the State*: computers, metadata, mass interception, intelligence databases and the enormous scale of modern surveillance. ([MoviesPack](https://moviespack.com/similar/enemy-of-the-state?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

12. **The Lives of Others** + **The Conversation** are particularly interesting as a pair.  
    Both essentially ask: **What happens to the person who spends his life watching other people?**

### 🕵️ The really paranoid 1970s stuff

If you're willing to go older, **this is where I'd really send you.** The early/mid-70s produced an extraordinary cluster of paranoid political thrillers in the aftermath of Watergate, Vietnam, CIA scandals, etc. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_thriller?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

- **All the President's Men (1976)** - All the President's Men - less espionage, but the Watergate machinery behind it is fascinating.
- **The Parallax View (1974)** - political assassination/conspiracy.
- **The Conversation (1974)** - surveillance.
- **Three Days of the Condor (1975)** - CIA internal conspiracy.
- **The Day of the Jackal (1973)** - assassination as an extremely professional clandestine operation.
- **Seven Days in May (1964)** - military conspiracy and an attempted coup against the U.S. president.
- **Blow Out (1981)** - surveillance/recording accidentally captures evidence of a political assassination.

The combination of **The Conversation → Three Days of the Condor → The Parallax View** is probably the closest thing to a trilogy of *Enemy of the State's* intellectual ancestors.

### 💣 And if you want the *action* side

If your favorite part was the relentless cat-and-mouse aspect rather than the political realism:

- **The Bourne Identity / Supremacy / Ultimatum** - CIA black programs, assassinations, rogue operations.
- **Ronin (1998)** - intelligence operatives, mercenaries, double-crosses and extremely believable tradecraft.
- **Mission: Impossible (1996)** - much more Hollywood, but the basic premise is an intelligence operation going catastrophically wrong.
- **Salt (2010)** - sleeper agents, CIA/KGB paranoia.
- **The International (2009)** - intelligence agencies, political influence and covert assassination.
- **Body of Lies (2008)** - CIA field operations, informants, torture and manipulation in the Middle East.
